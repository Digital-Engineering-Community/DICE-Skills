# Fact-Checking: Philosophical Context

Method descriptions (SIFT, IFCN, Lateral Reading, etc.) — in `methodologies.md`. This document covers the philosophical context underlying the skill's epistemic frameworks.

---

## Operational Criterion (Turing, 1950)

**Source:** Turing's "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" — the Turing Test assesses intelligence through observable outcomes, not internal mechanisms.

**For fact-checking:** a claim is verifiable if you can formulate a verification criterion in terms of an observable outcome. A fact is confirmed when verified through a primary source or independent sources. **Independent source** — does not share a common primary origin with another confirmation, is not a reprint or retelling, has no conflict of interest regarding the fact. A factoid is widely circulated but fails verification on inspection.

**Parallel with SIFT:** SIFT practices the same principle — verify through outcome (find better coverage), not through analysis of the claim itself.

## Falsificationism (Popper)

A theory is scientific not when it is confirmed, but when it can be refuted. Confirmation does not prove; one disconfirmation is sufficient. Practice: deliberately search for facts that would refute the hypothesis.

Applicability: use when the material has a clear argumentative structure or when the stakes are high enough that a false main claim would mislead. Skip for simple lookup tasks.

## Research Programmes (Lakatos)

Hard core (non-negotiable assumptions) + protective belt (auxiliary hypotheses). A progressive programme predicts new facts. A degenerative one only explains post hoc. Source assessment: does it predict something new, or only retrofit explanations after the fact?

Applicability: use only in publication-grade mode when the conflict is between competing methodological frameworks, not between data points.

## Paradigms (Kuhn)

Science develops through revolutions. Paradigms are incommensurable — their adherents speak different languages. Practice: when sources conflict radically, do not reduce to a common denominator. Instead, present both coordinate systems honestly.

## The Caulfield–Wineburg Connection

Caulfield (SIFT) and Wineburg (Lateral Reading) are co-authors of "Verified: How to Think Straight, Talk Better, and Build Your Knowledge" (University of Chicago Press). Two techniques described as independent were created by people working together.

## Verdict Classification

- **Supported** — confirmed by a reliable primary source or independent sources
- **Partially supported** — correct in essence, but with caveats
- **Unsupported** — insufficient data
- **Conflicting** — sources diverge
- **Refuted** — demonstrably false
- **Misleading** — not technically false, but distorts context
